Retail Reality Check

Every trade: EV = win_rate*avg_win − loss_rate*avg_loss − costs. Retail dies on the costs term and on overestimating the first two.

  • 70-90% of retail traders lose over any multi-year window; "winners" are often small-sample luck.
  • You're not vs "the market" — you're on the other side of faster, richer, better-informed participants. HFT/latency races are off-limits !
  • Real retail edges are structural or behavioural, not a magic indicator: patience, no redemptions, tiny illiquid corners funds can't fit into.
  • Anyone promising 5%/month is selling you something.

Capital matters

  • ~$2k: fixed costs & min sizes dominate; favour low-turnover.
  • ~$50k: room to diversify across a few edges.

Verify an edge: honest backtest → then paper-trade live for months. If live fills don't match backtest, the "edge" was an artefact.

TypeWhen
Vectorised (vectorbt/pandas)fast idea-screening, param sweeps; trivially easy to leak the future !

Screen in vectorbt, validate in backtrader. If they disagree wildly, the vectorised one leaked.

equity   = (1 + strat_r).cumprod()
run_max  = equity.cummax()
drawdown = equity/run_max - 1.0
max_dd   = drawdown.min()      # worst peak-to-trough
sharpe   = strat_r.mean()/strat_r.std()*np.sqrt(365)
  • .shift(1) is the whole ballgame: decide at t, get filled at t+1. Miss it and Sharpe goes magical & fake !
  • Model costs on every fill; keep a locked OOS set, run once.
  • Sharpe 0.7-1.5 after costs = genuinely good. Sharpe 3-4 + smooth curve = a bug (~99% look-ahead) !
  • Need multiple regimes + >~100 trades; SE(Sharpe) ~ 1/sqrt(N).

Overfitting & Walk-Forward

The quiet reason strategies that "worked on the backtest" lose live: you fitted the noise, and noise doesn't repeat.

  • Every parameter/rule/asset/date-range you tuned spends a degree of freedom fitting randomness. Hidden params count too !
  • Want dozens of independent trades per parameter. 10 params on 40 trades = guaranteed garbage.
  • Simpler generalises: a 2-param edge beats ten 8-param "edges".
# walk-forward = rolling OOS
for train, test in windows:      # train=365 test=90
    p = optimise(train)          # fit on past only
    oos.append(run(test, p))     # trade untouched
equity = stitch(oos)             # honest expectation
  • Multiple testing: try 200 ideas, one looks great by luck. Expected max Sharpe from N noise trials grows ~sqrt(2·ln N) in SE units.
  • Ask "why does this persist & who's on the other side?" before trusting any curve. Rummaged edges don't survive.
  • Re-using OOS after seeing it = it's now in-sample, worthless !

Trend Following & Momentum

Buy what's rising; hold till the trend breaks. Persists via under-reaction + funds benchmarked out of trends. Positive skew: many small losses, few huge wins.

fast = close.rolling(20).mean()
slow = close.rolling(100).mean()
sig  = (fast > slow).astype(int).shift(1)  # no lookahead
strat_r = sig * close.pct_change()
  • TSMOM (absolute): own past return > 0 → long; goes to cash in bears. XS (relative): rank a universe, long winners/short losers.
  • Vol-target size (target_vol/asset_vol) matters more than the entry signal.
  • Don't hunt one lucky lookback — ensemble 3-5 speeds. If ±10% on lookback breaks it, it's a fit !
  • Reality: diversified managed-futures Sharpe ~0.4-0.7; crypto single-asset shows 100%+ CAGR but 50-70% drawdowns. Failure mode is quitting in the flat stretch before the trend that pays.

Mean Reversion

Bet price stretched too far snaps back. z = (price − ma)/sd. Enter long z<−2, short z>+2, exit toward 0. Bollinger = ma ± 2sd; RSI<30 oversold.

  • Wins often, loses rarely but big (negative skew). High win rate (60-70%) hides a fat left tail !
  • Home turf: calm, range-bound, liquid. Dies in trends/crashes — "oversold" gets more oversold; every bounce is a knife.
  • Must have a stop or regime filter — running it in a trend with no filter is the #1 killer !
  • Trades a lot → costs are first-order: a few-bps edge is eaten by 0.1% round-trip. Use limit orders to earn, not pay, the spread.
  • "Oversold" ≠ "cheap." In a downtrend oversold is the new normal.

Pairs Trading & Stat Arb

Two tethered assets diverge → long laggard, short leader, market-neutral. A leash between two dogs.

b = sm.add_constant(price_b)
hedge = sm.OLS(price_a, b).fit().params[1]
spread = price_a - hedge*price_b
adf_stat, pval, *_ = adfuller(spread)   # pval<0.05 = stationary
  • Correlation ≠ cointegration. Correlation = returns wiggle together; cointegration = price levels stay tethered (the leash). Trade cointegration; correlation is just a pre-filter !
  • Spread z-score: short at z>+2, long at z<−2, exit |z|<0.5, hard stop |z|>3-4 or time stop.
  • Hedge ratio drifts — rolling window or Kalman; don't peek with full-sample fit.
  • Blowup case: spread keeps widening (relationship broke) — short the winner, long the loser, no leash. Never average down; small size + hard stop.
  • Costs double (two legs + borrow/funding). Market-neutral = neutral to market, not to the relationship.

Market Making & Grid

Quote both sides, capture the spread, don't bet direction. You rent immediacy; the game is what happens when flow isn't balanced.

micro = (bid*ask_size + ask*bid_size)/(bid_size+ask_size)
my_bid = fair - half_spread - skew   # skew leans vs inventory
my_ask = fair + half_spread - skew
  • Realised edge = quoted spread − fees − inventory losses from adverse moves — often a fraction of the headline spread.
  • Inventory risk dominates: in a trend takers keep hitting one side; you buy the falling knife. Skew quotes against inventory (Avellaneda-Stoikov formalises it).
  • Adverse selection: you get filled mainly when someone knows something ! On majors you lose the queue to colocated firms.
  • Retail angle: illiquid pairs pros ignore, grid trading (fixed ladder), maker rebates as structural edge. Backtested fills are where MM lies.

Breakout & Volatility

Markets coil (low vol, range) then release (trend). Be positioned for the release. Vol strategies trade move size, not direction.

  • Donchian: long when close > prior N-bar high (.shift(1)!), exit on symmetric low. Turtle = 20-day in / 10-day out.
  • ATR stops (2-3×ATR) normalise risk across assets/regimes; trailing exits let winners run.
  • Low win rate (30-40%), 2.5:1+ payoff — positive skew, you feel wrong constantly. Cutting winners early destroys the edge !
  • Only take breakouts from a squeeze (narrow bands/low ATR) — better follow-through than breaks from chop.
  • Costs bite: you enter on the noisiest bars (wide spread, slippage). Diversify across many uncorrelated markets to lift a sub-1 Sharpe.

Arbitrage for Retail (mostly carry)

Most "arb" retail can reach is really a carry trade with a tail, not risk-free.

FlavourReachable?
Cross-exchange (spatial)Dead — gap closes in ms; transfers take min-hrs + withdrawal limits !
Triangular (one venue)latency race you lose to bots; survivors too small to cover fees
Funding-rateYes — hold a position, collect funding
Cash-and-carry / basisYes — long spot, short premium future; basis→0 at expiry
  • Reachable ones are carry (held hours-months), not races. Real returns high-single-digit to ~20% APR, spiking in frenzies.
  • Biggest historic "arb" losses were exchanges failing while you had capital on them — counterparty risk is not zero !

Crypto: Funding, Perps & Basis

Perp = future with no expiry, glued to spot by the funding rate (paid every ~8h). Positive funding: longs pay shorts (bullish crowd).

Delta-neutral carry: long 1 BTC spot + short 1 BTC perp. Price cancels; short leg collects funding.

funding_pnl = notional * funding_rate    # per interval
# 0.01%/8h  ->  0.03%/day  ~=  11%/yr on notional
  • Realistic net 5-15% APR normal, 30%+ in euphoria (exactly when exchange/execution risk peaks). Capacity for retail ~unlimited.
  • You're paid because leveraged longs overpay for exposure. Funding mean-reverts & flips sign — can pay for weeks in bear/calm.
  • Footguns ! leverage on the "neutral" trade → short leg liquidated on a spike; legs on separate collateral; taker fees (0.02-0.06%/side/leg) eat weeks of funding; chasing illiquid-altcoin funding.

Crypto On-Chain: AMM / DEX / MEV

On-chain you are usually the prey. Edge = understanding that well enough not to get skinned.

# constant product x*y=k
def amm_out(res_in, res_out, amt_in, fee=0.003):
    af = amt_in*(1-fee)
    return res_out - (res_in*res_out)/(res_in+af)
  • Bigger trade vs pool → further up the curve → more slippage (deterministic given reserves).
  • Impermanent loss: if the price ratio moves, arbers rebalance the pool at your expense; IL often exceeds fees earned unless stable-stable !
  • Sandwich (MEV): bot buys before your pending tx, you fill worse, it sells after — pockets your slippage. Use private RPC / MEV-protection, tight slippage tolerance !
  • Mainnet gas can make small swaps absurd ($15 gas on a $50 swap = 30% haircut). On-chain data is slow public context, not fast alpha.

Edges That Survive for Retail

You win on edges that exist because of small size. A real edge has a reason it persists — a cost, constraint, or behaviour. Can't name it? Assume it's fake !

FamilyExample
Capacity-constrainedmicro-caps, thin crypto pairs too small for funds
Structural nichefunding carry, cross-exchange fragmentation, stablecoin depeg reversion
Behavioural/calendarTSMOM, turn-of-month, post-earnings drift
Speed-agnosticanything held days-to-weeks (never race HFT)
  • Capacity ceiling is the point & the limit: profit is small in absolute $ and you can't compound past it. 15% on $20k = $3k.
  • "Being your own boss" edges: no redemptions, no mandate, no benchmark, no career risk — enablers that let you hold through pain funds can't.
  • A risk premium (funding, illiquidity, selling vol) is real return for bearing a bad day — not free money. Over-lever it and the tail wipes you.
  • Stack a few weakly-correlated real edges; retire one when its one-line thesis stops being true.

Signals & Features

A signal is a hypothesis encoded as a number. Nearly every classic indicator is a transform of the same price series — stacking ten adds correlated noise, not evidence !

  • MACD = diff of two MAs; Bollinger = MA ± vol; RSI/stochastics/ROC all measure the same momentum stretch. Check the correlation matrix — 0.8+ everywhere = one signal, not ten.
  • Good feature: stationary (use returns/z-scores, not raw price), economically motivated, not already priced in.
  • Combine a few weakly-correlated, individually-justified features (trend + mean-reversion + regime filter), not indicator soup.
  • Reality: on liquid markets most indicator edges are ~zero after costs (public data). A good signal might yield Sharpe 0.5-1.0 before it decays.

The ML Angle (and its traps)

ML is a magnifying glass, not a divining rod. It sharpens a faint real edge; with no edge it hallucinates one that evaporates live.

  • Build X (features/bar) & y (e.g. sign of next return). Split chronologically, walk-forward. Simple first: logistic → gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost/LightGBM). DL rarely beats trees on tabular financial data at retail scale.
  • Classification (up/flat/down with a dead-zone) > regression — magnitude is un-fittable noise. 53% directional can pay; 51% is overfit noise once costs bite.
  • Evaluate on net PnL vs buy-and-hold, not accuracy.
  • Killers ! random splits (shuffle leaks time), whole-dataset normalisation (leaks future stats), close of bar t predicting bar t, testing 200 combos and reporting the best.
  • Ceiling is low: best case Sharpe 0.8→1.0. Retail use = filter/sizer on a rule you can explain in one sentence. Expect live ~half of backtest.

Risk & Position Sizing

The topic that keeps you alive. Most losers had an edge — they sized too big and a normal losing streak ruined them.

risk_amt = equity * 0.01              # 1% default
stop_dist = abs(entry - stop)
size = risk_amt / stop_dist           # units
# widen stop_dist for slippage or you lose >1%

Kelly & vol-target

kelly_f = p - (1-p)/b       # p=win prob, b=payoff ratio
# 55% & b=1 -> 0.10 ; use 1/4-1/2 Kelly, never full
weight = target_vol / asset_vol       # 0.15/0.60 = 0.25x
  • Risk 0.5-2% per trade (1% default): a 10-loss streak costs ~10%, survivable. Full Kelly = 40-50% drawdowns & assumes you know your edge !
  • Stop where the thesis is wrong (ATR-based), not a round number crowds cluster at (stop-hunted).
  • Hard drawdown limit (−20-30%) forces a pause, not revenge trading. Never average down. A 50% DD needs a 100% gain back.
  • "Diversified" altcoins = one crypto-beta bet; correlations →1 in a crash. Size for the crash.

Execution, Fees & Slippage

Quietly decides whether a winning backtest is a losing strategy. Round-trip ≈ fees + spread + slippage.

annual_cost ~= turnover * round_trip_cost
# 0.15% RT, daily (252x) -> ~38%/yr hurdle
# weekly (52x) -> ~8% ; monthly (12x) -> ~2%
  • Maker (resting limit, low/rebate fee) vs taker (aggressive, higher, crosses spread). Prefer maker where you can wait; taker for stops/exits where certainty wins.
  • Marketable limit = near-instant fill + worst-case price cap.
  • Costs dominate at retail not because you're charged more, but because cheap-to-access edges are thin — pick edges large vs your ~0.15% RT.
  • Your stop is your most expensive fill (taker, adverse, fast book) — budget slippage into sizing !
  • Model costs on every fill; sensitivity-sweep at 2-3×. Survives only at optimistic costs = dead.
  • Perp funding held overnight is a recurring cost — include it.

Automation & Bedroom Infra

Most blowups aren't a bad signal — they're a script that died at 3am, an order that double-fired, or a leaked key. Keep the plumbing boring.

ArchUse
Cron (fetch→signal→trade→exit)daily/hourly; crash recovery free (next run fresh). Default.
Always-on bot (websocket)only for MM / tight intraday; you own reconnects & state
  • Run on a $5-10/mo VPS (Hetzner/DO/Vultr), not a laptop that sleeps. systemd/pm2 restart on crash; SSH keys only.
  • Idempotency: attach a client order ID so retries don't duplicate orders !
  • Reconciliation: compare what the bot thinks it holds vs what the exchange reports.
  • Secrets in git-ignored .env — bots scrape public repos for keys within minutes ! Restrict keys to trade-only.
  • Heartbeat/dead-man alert (Telegram) that fires when the bot goes silent, not just on error.

Psychology & Taxes

Two quiet edge-leaks that can each halve a modest edge.

Discipline is mechanical, not heroic

  • Automate execution so there's no click to second-guess. Pre-commit rules in writing.
  • You override right when it matters most — killing a mean-reversion trade just before it reverts ! Track override P&L separately; your gut usually costs money.
  • Revenge trading (bigger unplanned bet to win a loss back) is the #1 account-killer — accounts die from the sequence after a loss, not the loss.
  • Size down after wins / up after losses? Backwards. Journal every trade in R (units risked).

Tax reality

  • Every disposal is taxable — crypto-to-crypto swaps included ! Keep cost-basis records from trade one (Koinly/CoinTracker).
  • Short-term churn is taxed higher than long holds in many jurisdictions — after-tax return is what you keep. Mind wash-sale rules.

★ Pre-Live / Paper-Trade Checklist

Backtest honesty

  • ☐ .shift(1) on every signal; execute next bar, not the signal bar's close.
  • ☐ Costs (fee + spread + slippage) on every fill; sensitivity-swept at 2-3×.
  • ☐ Point-in-time universe (delisted names / full historical listing); prices split-adjusted.
  • ☐ Locked OOS run once; walk-forward stitched curve holds up.

Edge sanity

  • ☐ One-line thesis: why does it persist & who's on the other side?
  • ☐ Trial count honest; survivor beats the noise-max Sharpe; ≤3 params.
  • ☐ Survives on a different asset / doubled costs; turnover hurdle cleared.

Risk & ops

  • ☐ Fixed 0.5-1% risk/trade; real pre-decided stop (slippage-padded); ±20-30% kill limit.
  • ☐ Vol-targeted; correlated positions treated as one bet; low/no leverage.
  • ☐ Keys env-only & no-withdraw; client order IDs; reconciliation + heartbeat alert.

Forward test

  • ☐ Paper-traded weeks-to-months across ≥2 regimes; live ≈ backtest distribution.
  • ☐ No mid-test retuning; kill criteria written before going live.
